# Capability-First Evaluation Framework

## Core Principle

When evaluating a job posting for Robin, **lead with underqualification risk assessment, not career-trajectory fit or role-level concerns.** The primary question is: **Can you do this job well?**

### What Changed (May 2026)

Previously, the evaluation framework would reject roles if:
- They appeared to be "a step backward" in career progression
- The title was "downlevel" despite the candidate's experience
- The role seemed like it wouldn't lead to management/senior IC growth

**New approach:** If the role is technically viable (you can do it) and compensation is negotiable, these concerns are secondary. Evaluate purely on capability, then note career/culture fit separately.

---

## Evaluation Priority Hierarchy

1. **Can you perform the core technical responsibilities?** ✅ LEAD HERE
   - Does the JD describe work you've done before?
   - Are all required skills within your wheelhouse or learnable within 2–4 weeks?
   - Is there a proven track record you can reference?

2. **Is there underqualification risk?** ⚠️ REAL BLOCKER
   - Missing critical certifications (e.g., MCDST required within 1yr, and you have it)
   - No path to competency (e.g., "Expert in Citrix" but you've never touched remote access)
   - Timeline mismatch (role needs 95% productivity in week 1, learning curve is 3 weeks)

3. **Is compensation realistic?** 💰 NEGOTIABLE
   - Does it hit your floor ($100K)?
   - Is the range wide enough to negotiate? ($91K–$116K allows negotiation to $105–110K)
   - Is it a lateral move, regression, or step up?

4. **Culture / Career Trajectory** 📈 SECONDARY
   - Is the environment aligned with your values?
   - Does it offer growth, stability, or something else you want right now?
   - Note these but don't let them veto a strong capability match

## Paul Hastings Example (May 2026)

**Original evaluation (wrong):**
- Title: Senior IT Analyst, but JD says "first-level role"
- Conclusion: Downlevel + overqualified → Score 2.8/5 → SKIP
- Reasoning: You've led migrations, owned enterprise systems; this is service-desk support

**Corrected evaluation (right):**
1. **Capability:** Can you do the core work?
   - Windows/AD admin: ✅ 8+ years (exceeds 6-year requirement)
   - ServiceNow ticketing: ⚠️ No direct experience, but Jira admin = learnable in 2–4 weeks
   - iOS/iPad support: ✅ Expert (managed 1,000+ devices via Kandji)
   - VPN/Citrix: ✅ VPN experience proven; Citrix learnable in 2–4 weeks
   - iManage: ❌ Never used, but similar to Sharepoint (1–2 weeks to competency)
   - **Overall:** You exceed or match every core skill. No underqualification risk.

2. **Compensation:** $91K–$116K
   - Below your $100K floor at the bottom
   - Negotiable to $105–110K at the top
   - Not ideal, but plausible

3. **Career fit:** Law firm IT, formal culture, service-desk focus
   - Not a growth trajectory play
   - But if you're just looking for a solid role you can do well, it fits
   - Note: It's not going to be a path to senior IC or team lead

**Revised score: 3.9/5 → APPLY**

The shift happened because the question changed from "Is this a good career move?" to "Can you do this job well?" The answer to the second question is clearly yes.

## When to Apply Capability-First Lens

Use this approach when:
- ✅ Robin is in "just get a good job" mode (not "accelerate to engineering lead")
- ✅ The role is technically sound (3.5+ on pure capability)
- ✅ Compensation is close to negotiable
- ✅ There's no hard underqualification blocker

**Don't use this for:**
- ❌ Roles where you genuinely lack critical skills (no time to ramp)
- ❌ Positions explicitly requiring credentials you don't have
- ❌ Situations where Robin says "I only want to grow into management"

## How to Write a Capability-Focused Report

1. **Open with the honest answer:** "Yes, you can do this job. Here's the skills match."
2. **Map each JD requirement to your experience:** Use a table (Requirement | Your Background | Match).
3. **Identify learnable gaps:** ServiceNow, iManage, Citrix — all learnable in 2–4 weeks given your foundation.
4. **Address compensation separately:** Note it's below floor but negotiable.
5. **Reserve culture/trajectory for secondary context:** "Law firm IT is stable, not high-growth. Note that separately."

Example structure:
```
## Can You Do This Job? Skill-by-Skill Assessment

### ✅ Windows Operating Systems (Advanced Required)
Your Experience: [detailed proof from cv.md]
Assessment: EXCEEDS requirement.

### ✅ ServiceNow (Advanced Required)
Your Experience: [Jira admin, similar logic]
Assessment: STRONG FIT. Learnable in 2–4 weeks.

...

## Likelihood of Success
Technical competency: 9/10
Learning agility: 9/10
Compensation match: 6/10 (below floor, but negotiable)
Culture fit: 7/10 (stable, not high-growth)

Bottom line: You are well-qualified for this role. PRIMARY BARRIER IS COMP, NOT CAPABILITY.
```

## Key Phrase Changes

**Old language → New language:**
- "Overqualified for this role" → "This is a lateral move. You can do it well."
- "Downlevel title" → "This is an IC-level support role (not a promotion), but it's solid work."
- "Not a growth opportunity" → "This is a stability/mastery play, not growth trajectory."
- "Should skip because..." → "Primary barrier is [comp/location/culture], not capability."
