# I Let AI Run My Job Search (And It Actually Worked)

I'm Robin. Senior IT support specialist based in Fremont, CA. I've spent years making sure other people's technology works — laptops, networks, conference rooms, the CEO's phone at 6am. I'm good at it.

But when it was time to find my next job? I was a mess.

Tabs everywhere. Spreadsheets I stopped updating after week two. Applications I forgot I sent. Recruiters emailing me about roles I had zero memory of. The job search is its own full-time job, and nobody tells you that until you're already drowning in it.

So I did what any IT person would do: I built a system.

---

## The Problem Every Job Seeker Knows

Whether you're in tech or not, job searching in 2025 feels like this:

You find a posting. You spend an hour tailoring your resume. You apply. You hear nothing for three weeks. Then they email you about a completely different role and you have to dig through your email history to remember what you even applied for.

Multiply that by 30 applications and you're exhausted before you even get to the interview stage.

The problem isn't effort. It's organization. And it's the fact that most job postings are vague, repetitive, or just not right for you — but you can't always tell until you've already invested time in them.

I wanted a way to filter faster, apply smarter, and actually remember what I was doing.

---

## What I Built

I set up an AI-powered job search pipeline on my laptop. No subscriptions, no fancy software. Just a few open-source tools working together.

Here's what it does in plain English:

**It reads job postings for me.** I drop in a link or a job description, and the AI reads it, compares it to my resume and what I'm actually looking for, and gives it a score from 1 to 5. A 4 or above means it's worth my time. Below that, it tells me why it's not a good fit.

**It writes tailored resumes.** Not generic ones. It reads the specific job description and adjusts how my experience is framed to match what that company is actually asking for. I review it before it goes anywhere — the AI drafts, I decide.

**It tracks everything.** Every job I look at gets logged automatically — company, role, score, whether I applied, what stage I'm at. I have a live dashboard on my laptop that shows my whole pipeline at a glance. No more mystery spreadsheets.

**It preps me for interviews.** Before a recruiter call, I can ask the system to research the company and pull together likely interview questions based on the role. It knows my work history and gives me talking points I can actually use.

**It scans for new jobs without me asking.** It checks the career pages of companies I'm interested in every few days and flags anything new that matches my targets. I wake up to a shortlist instead of spending an hour on job boards.

---

## The Part That Surprised Me

I expected the resume tailoring to be the most useful part. It's not.

The most useful part is the scoring.

Before I built this, I applied to everything that looked vaguely possible. Hope for the best. The problem with that strategy is that it dilutes your energy. You end up sending mediocre applications everywhere instead of strong applications to the right places.

Now the AI talks me out of bad applications. It will literally say: "This role is asking for X and Y, which aren't in your profile. Score: 2.8. I'd recommend skipping this one." And most of the time, it's right.

It's like having a brutally honest career coach who has read every job description you've ever looked at and knows your resume better than you do.

---

## What This Looks Like Day to Day

On a typical week:

- New job postings land in my pipeline automatically from portal scans or when I forward a recruiter email
- The AI evaluates each one overnight
- I open my dashboard in the morning and see what scored well
- I click into the report for anything interesting — it's a full breakdown of why the role is or isn't a fit
- For the good ones, I approve a tailored resume and it generates a PDF
- Before any interview, I run a prep session and get company research plus likely questions

The whole thing takes maybe 30 minutes of my active time per day instead of 3 hours of scattered, anxious job searching.

## You Don't Have to Be in IT to Use This

I want to be honest: setting this up took some technical effort on my end. I work in IT, so that's my comfort zone.

But the underlying tool — called Career-Ops, created by Santiago Fernández de Valderrama — is open source and designed to work with any AI assistant. Santiago used it to evaluate 740 job offers and land a Head of AI role. The system has been adapted for job seekers in all kinds of fields, not just tech.

If you're not technical, the concepts still apply even if you don't run the exact same setup:

- Use ChatGPT or Claude to score job postings against your resume before you apply
- Ask AI to tailor your resume for each role instead of sending the same one everywhere
- Keep a simple spreadsheet tracker — even a basic one beats nothing
- Let AI prep you for interviews by researching the company and generating practice questions

The goal is the same whether you're automated or doing it manually: fewer, better applications. Quality over volume.

---

## The Honest Truth About AI and Job Searching

AI doesn't get you the job. You do.

What it does is remove the noise so you can focus on what matters — the conversations, the interviews, the relationships. The parts that actually require a human.

I still write my own cover letters. I still review every resume before it goes out. I still decide what to apply for. The system doesn't replace my judgment — it gives my judgment better information to work with.

And honestly? It's made the job search feel less like drowning and more like running a project I'm actually in control of.
